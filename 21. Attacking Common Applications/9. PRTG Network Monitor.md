## Leveraging Known Vulnerabilities
- Find out the version number and check for known CVE's.
- we can explore a bit, but we know that this is likely vulnerable to a command injection flaw

> This excellent [blog post](https://www.codewatch.org/blog/?p=453) by the individual who discovered this flaw does a great job of walking through the initial discovery process and how they discovered it.

**Step 1** : To begin, mouse over `Setup` in the top right and then the `Account Settings` menu and finally click on `Notifications`.
![[Pasted image 20260713090337.png]]
**Step 2** :  click on `Add new notification`
![[Pasted image 20260713090414.png]]
- Give the notification a name and scroll down and ticck the box next to `EXECUTE PROGRAM`.

**Step 3** : Under `Program File`, select `Demo exe notification - outfile.ps1` from the drop-down. Finally, in the parameter field, enter a command. For our purposes, we will add a new local admin user by entering `test.txt;net user prtgadm1 Pwn3d_by_PRTG! /add;net localgroup administrators prtgadm1 /add`.

- During an actual assessment, we may want to do something that does not change the system, such as getting a reverse shell or connection to our favorite C2.
**Step 4** : After clicking `Save`, we will be redirected to the `Notifications` page and see our new notification named `pwn` in the list.

**Step 5** : You can s`chedule the notification to run later`, making it useful for persistence. After configuring it, click **Test** to execute the command. If successful, you'll see **"EXE notification is queued up."** If an error appears, recheck the notification settings.
